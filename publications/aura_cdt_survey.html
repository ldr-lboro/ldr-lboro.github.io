🟢
PhD topic and research projects are the primary draw
The topic/scope of the CDT (avg +1.60) and quality of information about research projects (+1.40) are the strongest pull factors — consistently rated positively across almost all respondents. Applicants are motivated by the subject matter first.
🟢
Career pathways and industry engagement are highly valued
Opportunities for a future industry career, future academic career, and stakeholder/industry engagement all scored +1.10. The prospect of making a tangible impact through an industry-connected PhD is a major motivator.
🟡
Stipend and cohort community are moderate positives
Financial considerations (avg +0.80) and cohort building opportunities (+0.80) are meaningful but inconsistent pull factors. One respondent explicitly suggested that better communication of the stipend's tax-free status and 4-year tenure could strengthen this further.
🟡
Recruitment process and website are appreciated but could do more work
The recruitment process (+0.60) and CDT website (+0.50) were broadly positive, but one respondent found project listings unclear about physical location. A cleaner distinction of where each project is based could reduce confusion at the point of application.
🔴
Hull relocation is the clearest drawback
Being based at Hull for the first year is the only category with a negative average score (−0.50) and was rated negatively by 4 of 9 applicable respondents. Several noted it is a real barrier for prospective applicants, even if manageable once committed.
🔴
Geographic location and partner universities are weak or polarising
Geographic location averaged just +0.10 with split views, and partner universities scored +0.30 but with two 'Much less' ratings — suggesting that for some, institutional associations can actively deter. Academic supervisor credibility appears to matter more than institutional brand.
💡 Signal from open comments
Multiple respondents highlighted that attending webinars, careers fairs and talks by current cohort students — and having clear project listings on the website — were influential. Boosting visibility through events and improving listing clarity (especially around project location) appear to be low-effort, high-return interventions.
